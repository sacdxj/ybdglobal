# Next Phase Operating Plan

This document defines the next operating phase after the core S-level, A-level product, application, and capability pages have reached the current procurement-decision standard.

The next phase should not focus on rewriting page copy. The priority is to add real evidence, build engineering knowledge assets, and validate RFQ conversion with controlled paid search.

## 1. Phase Goal

Move the website from:

```text
Professional procurement-oriented website
```

to:

```text
Evidence-backed engineering supplier platform
```

Primary outcome:

- More qualified RFQs with drawings, samples, quantity range, company email, and application details.

Secondary outcomes:

- Stronger buyer confidence.
- Stronger topical authority.
- Better Google Ads landing-page validation.
- Clearer internal operating rhythm for sales, engineering, photography, and content.

## 2. Current Page Status

The current website structure is strong enough for first-stage SEO and Google Ads landing-page use.

Current focus:

- Do not keep rewriting core page copy unless real evidence or real data requires it.
- Use real assets to replace conceptual proof.
- Use knowledge content to answer engineering search intent.
- Use Google Ads to validate which page and offer creates useful RFQs.

## 3. Evidence Asset Priority

Evidence should reduce procurement risk. Do not collect random factory photos. Collect assets that answer a buyer's hidden question:

Can this supplier really make, inspect, approve, package, and repeat this part?

## 5. Photo and Video Shot List

### 5.1 Photo Rules

- Use real parts and real process scenes.
- Keep backgrounds clean and industrial.
- Shoot close-ups that show fit, surface, thread, finish, and packaging.
- Avoid overly polished consumer-product styling.
- Do not imply a photo is a certification or test result unless the document is real and approved for publication.

### 5.2 Required Shots

| Shot Type | Minimum Quantity | Notes |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Product hero close-ups | 12 | Binding posts first, then banana plugs, spades, high-current terminals |
| CNC / automatic lathe process | 8 | Include machine, workholding, part-in-process, finished pieces |
| Inspection process | 10 | Caliper, micrometer, thread gauge, visual inspection, sample comparison |
| Brass material / parts | 8 | Bar stock, threaded hardware, plating-ready parts |
| Copper material / parts | 8 | Contact surfaces, current-path parts, burr-control view |
| Injection molded insulation | 8 | Red/black caps, washers, bushings, fit with metal parts |
| Plating / finish samples | 8 | Contact zones, appearance zones, masked/protected areas |
| Assembly and packaging | 10 | Kits, paired sets, labels, bags, cartons |
| RFQ / drawing review scene | 4 | Desk review, drawing markup, sample comparison |

### 5.3 Video Clips

Short clips should be 8 to 20 seconds:

- CNC turning terminal hardware.
- Thread inspection.
- Copper contact surface inspection.
- Red/black insulation fit with metal part.
- Assembly of binding post set.
- Packaging of paired terminal hardware.

Use clips as supporting evidence on pages and for LinkedIn / YouTube later.

### 6.2 Article Format

Each article should include:

1. Buyer question.
2. Short answer.
3. Decision framework.
4. Practical table or checklist.
5. Common failure risks.
6. Related product / application / capability links.
7. RFQ CTA.

Avoid:

- Company news.
- Generic definitions with no sourcing value.
- Unsupported performance claims.
- Consumer audiophile language as the main frame.

## 7. Google Ads Validation Plan

Google Ads should validate RFQ intent and page performance. It should not be treated as the long-term growth engine by itself.

### 7.1 Test Budget and Goal

Initial test:

- Run a small controlled search campaign.
- Focus only on high-intent manufacturer / custom / OEM terms.
- Measure qualified RFQ quality, not clicks.

Primary validation question:

```text
Which keyword + landing page combination creates qualified RFQs?
```

### 7.5 Qualified RFQ Definition

A qualified RFQ should include at least three of the following:

- Drawing, sample, or detailed reference.
- Application or equipment type.
- Quantity range.
- Material or finish requirement.
- Company email.
- Project stage.
- Target timeline.

## 9. Decision Rules

### When to Update Page Copy Again

Only update core page copy when:

- Real evidence reveals a stronger proof point.
- Ads or analytics show buyer confusion.
- RFQ questions repeat the same missing information.
- A real case study or project example becomes publishable.

### When Not to Update Page Copy

Do not keep rewriting copy because:

- A headline could sound slightly better.
- Another competitor uses a different phrase.
- A page feels new and tempting to polish.
- The team has no new proof or data.

At this stage, new evidence and market validation matter more than copy polish.
