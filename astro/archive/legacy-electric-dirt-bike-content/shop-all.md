## What to Compare Beyond Top Speed

| Decision | Why it matters | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Physical fit | A bike can be too tall, heavy, or long even when its power sounds appropriate | Dimensions, seat height, bike weight, approved rider guidance |
| Control | Usable power matters more than a peak number | Riding modes, throttle response, gearing, controller behavior |
| Braking and suspension | Speed is useful only when the chassis can manage the terrain | Component specifications and intended use |
| Ride time | One range number cannot describe every ride | Battery capacity, test conditions, terrain and rider assumptions |
| Charging | Charging affects daily use and storage | Approved charger, charge time, electrical requirements |
| Support | Similar specs do not mean similar ownership | Warranty, parts, manuals, service and contact path |

## Not Sure Which Bike Fits?

Use this order:

1. Measure the rider.
2. Define the main riding location and terrain.
3. Choose a controllable performance class.
4. Compare verified battery, range, brakes, suspension, and tires.
5. Confirm shipping, assembly, parts, and warranty.

## Model Comparison FAQ

### Are all electric dirt bikes suitable for adults?

No. Product dimensions, bike weight, rider guidance, performance, and intended use vary by
model. Use the adult collection and the exact product specifications.

### Can I choose a kids bike by age?

Age can be a starting point, but it is not enough. Measure the rider and consider experience,
controls, brakes, supervision, protective gear, and riding location.

### Does higher voltage always mean a faster bike?

No. Voltage is one part of a complete drive system. Motor, controller, battery, gearing, weight,
tires, software, and test conditions also matter.

### Are these bikes street legal?

Do not assume so. Product documentation, vehicle classification, equipment, and state/local
rules determine whether a specific bike can be used on public roads.
