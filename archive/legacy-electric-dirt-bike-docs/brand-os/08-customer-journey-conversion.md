# Customer Journey And Conversion System

## Conversion Philosophy

The brand should not push every visitor directly to checkout.

The conversion path should match the buyer's confidence level.

```text
Unclear buyer -> decision guide
Comparing buyer -> comparison or collection
Ready buyer -> product page
Concerned buyer -> support proof
High-risk buyer -> contact or review path
```

## Journey Stages

### Stage 1: Problem Recognition

Buyer thought:

```text
I want an electric dirt bike, but I do not know where to start.
```

Content:

- Homepage.
- Buying guides hub.
- Best electric dirt bikes.
- Adult/kids starting pages.

CTA:

- Start Your Buying Decision.
- Choose by Rider.

### Stage 2: Fit Discovery

Buyer thought:

```text
What type of bike fits this rider and use case?
```

Content:

- Adult guide.
- Kids and youth guide.
- Terrain-use guide.
- Voltage guides.

CTA:

- Compare Models.
- Find a Kids Bike.
- Compare Adult Models.

### Stage 3: Spec And Proof Comparison

Buyer thought:

```text
Which specs matter, and which claims can I trust?
```

Content:

- 60V versus 72V.
- Range guide.
- Battery and charging.
- Motor power.
- Product pages.

CTA:

- View Proof Checklist.
- Compare Models.
- Ask Support.

### Stage 4: Competitor Comparison

Buyer thought:

```text
How does this compare with Surron, Talaria, Razor, or another brand?
```

Content:

- Comparison hub.
- Competitor alternative pages.

CTA:

- Compare by Use Case.
- View Matching Models.
- Ask Before Choosing.

### Stage 5: Purchase Confidence

Buyer thought:

```text
What happens after I order?
```

Content:

- Shipping.
- Warranty.
- Financing.
- Parts.
- Repair.
- Contact.

CTA:

- Confirm Shipping.
- Ask Support.
- Request Current Availability.

### Stage 6: Post-Purchase Trust

Buyer thought:

```text
How do I assemble, charge, maintain, and get support?
```

Content:

- Setup guidance.
- Charging.
- Maintenance.
- Parts.
- Warranty claim process.

CTA:

- Contact Support.
- Find Parts.
- Review Maintenance Steps.

## CTA Hierarchy

### Primary CTAs

- Start Your Buying Decision.
- Compare Models.
- Find the Right Fit.
- Ask Support.

### Secondary CTAs

- Read the Guide.
- Compare Adult Models.
- Find a Kids Bike.
- Check Street-Legal Requirements.
- Review Shipping and Warranty.

### Avoid As Primary CTAs

- Buy Now, unless proof and checkout are ready.
- Shop Now, unless the buyer is clearly on a ready-to-buy page.
- Request Quote, unless product price and sales process require quotes.

## Trust Assets

The site needs these assets to convert as a decision brand:

- Rider-fit checklist.
- Youth safety checklist.
- Terrain-use matrix.
- Voltage comparison matrix.
- Range method explainer.
- Shipping process.
- Warranty process.
- Parts and repair workflow.
- Legal-document checklist.
- Product proof table.
- Comparison criteria.

## Page-Level Conversion Rules

### Homepage

Goal:

Move the buyer from confusion to a decision path.

Primary CTA:

Start Your Buying Decision.

### Buying Guides

Goal:

Teach the decision method.

CTA:

Compare Models or continue to a specific guide.

### Product Pages

Goal:

Confirm fit and readiness.

CTA:

Request current price or compare.

### Support Pages

Goal:

Reduce ownership risk.

CTA:

Ask Support.

### Comparison Pages

Goal:

Help buyer compare without manipulation.

CTA:

Compare by use case.

## Conversion Metrics

Do not judge only by sales.

Track:

- Guide entry clicks.
- Product comparison clicks.
- Support page visits before product visits.
- Contact/support inquiries.
- Availability requests.
- Model comparison engagement.
- Email capture from guides.
- Returning visitors.
- Organic landing pages that lead to product comparison.
- High-quality dealer/wholesale inquiries.

## Conversion Guardrail

If conversion copy increases urgency but decreases trust, it is not aligned with the brand.
