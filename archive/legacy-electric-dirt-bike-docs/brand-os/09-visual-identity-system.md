# Visual Identity System

## Visual Strategy

The visual identity should communicate confidence, outdoor capability, technical clarity, and
trust.

It should not look like:

- A toy bike brand.
- A bargain marketplace.
- A generic powersports blog.
- A dark extreme-sports hype brand.
- A thin affiliate review site.

## Visual Personality

- Modern outdoor.
- Clear and structured.
- Practical.
- Premium but approachable.
- Technical enough to support trust.
- Energetic without being reckless.

## Logo Criteria

The logo should:

- Work without an electric dirt bike icon.
- Be readable at small sizes.
- Work on product decals, helmets, apparel, website header, YouTube thumbnails, and packaging.
- Avoid overused lightning bolts, wheels, dirt splashes, and generic mountain icons unless treated
  with restraint.
- Support an abstract consumer brand name.

## Color Direction

Preferred color roles:

- Deep neutral for trust and readability.
- Warm action color for decisions and CTAs.
- Outdoor accent color for terrain and exploration.
- Safety/status colors for proof, caution, and review states.

Avoid:

- One-note neon green.
- Excessive black/red extreme-sports styling.
- Overused blue/purple tech gradients.
- Toy-like bright palettes.

## Typography

Typography should feel:

- Clear.
- Modern.
- Durable.
- Readable on mobile.
- Suitable for guides and comparison tables.

Avoid:

- Aggressive racing fonts.
- Novelty tech fonts.
- Overly condensed display fonts.
- Small body text in decision-heavy sections.

## Photography Direction

Use real or realistic product and riding imagery whenever possible.

Image categories:

- Rider fit.
- Family/parent decision.
- Trail and private-property use.
- Product detail.
- Battery and charging.
- Maintenance and parts.
- Delivery and support.
- Legal/document checklist.

Avoid:

- Dark, vague, atmospheric stock images.
- Unsafe riding.
- Unverified street use.
- Children riding without safety framing.
- Fake racing scenes that overpromise performance.

## UI Direction

The UI should feel like a decision tool.

Use:

- Comparison tables.
- Checklists.
- Fit cards.
- Proof status labels.
- Product filters.
- Scenario selectors.
- Guide cards.
- Caution notes.
- Support process steps.

Avoid:

- Pure marketing hero pages.
- Giant lifestyle sections without decision value.
- Cards that only decorate.
- Claims without proof state.

## Packaging And Product Extension

The visual system should work beyond the website:

- Product decals.
- Helmet boxes.
- Battery packaging.
- Charger labels.
- Manual covers.
- Warranty cards.
- Apparel tags.
- Dealer sheets.
- YouTube thumbnails.

## Visual Proof Standard

For product pages, use authentic product images before public launch.

Placeholder visuals are acceptable for internal prototypes only.

## Design Review Questions

- Does this make the brand feel trustworthy?
- Does this help the buyer compare or decide?
- Does this look durable enough for high-ticket products?
- Does this avoid toy-like or reckless cues?
- Does this work beyond bikes?
