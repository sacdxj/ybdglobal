# Brand Personality And Voice

## Brand As A Person

The brand is not a racer, influencer, or salesperson.

The brand is an experienced off-road coach:

- Around 45 years old.
- Has ridden and repaired bikes.
- Understands parents, beginners, and performance riders.
- Knows when speed is exciting and when it is the wrong starting point.
- Speaks directly.
- Does not oversell.
- Explains tradeoffs clearly.
- Cares about the rider after the sale.

## Personality Traits

- Experienced.
- Honest.
- Practical.
- Calm.
- Protective when safety matters.
- Confident without hype.
- Technical without being confusing.
- Commercial without being pushy.

## Voice Principles

### Be Direct

Say what the buyer should check first.

Good:

```text
Start with rider fit before comparing voltage.
```

Weak:

```text
Explore our amazing lineup of high-powered machines.
```

### Explain Tradeoffs

Every important spec has tradeoffs.

Good:

```text
Higher voltage can support stronger performance, but it does not replace fit, braking, suspension,
battery capacity, and rider skill.
```

### Separate Proof From Possibility

Good:

```text
This page needs final range testing before we use range as a headline claim.
```

### Respect Parent Anxiety

Good:

```text
Do not choose a youth bike by age alone. Check rider height, inseam, reach, experience,
protective gear, supervision, and riding area.
```

### Stay Useful

Every paragraph should help the buyer decide, verify, compare, or avoid a mistake.

## Words To Use Often

- Choose.
- Compare.
- Verify.
- Fit.
- Terrain.
- Rider.
- Control.
- Support.
- Ownership.
- Confidence.
- Evidence.
- Safety.
- Practical.
- Tradeoff.
- Before checkout.

## Words To Avoid Or Limit

- Amazing.
- Ultimate.
- Number one.
- Unbeatable.
- Game-changing.
- Fastest.
- Safest.
- Guaranteed legal.
- Best for everyone.
- Perfect for all ages.
- No maintenance.
- Zero risk.

## Copy Rules

- Lead with the buyer's decision, not the product feature.
- Use "best for" and "not ideal for" together.
- Explain how to verify claims.
- Include safety and legal caveats where relevant.
- Use tables and checklists for decision-heavy topics.
- Avoid fake urgency.
- Avoid forced competitor comparisons.
- Avoid unsupported promises.

## Page Tone By Type

### Homepage

Calm, confident, brand-level.

### Product Pages

Specific, proof-aware, conversion-focused, with clear fit and limits.

### Buying Guides

Educational, direct, structured, useful.

### Comparison Pages

Objective, careful, transparent, non-attacking.

### Street-Legal Pages

Cautious, official-source-driven, review-required.

### Kids And Youth Pages

Parent-safe, supervision-aware, non-hype.

### Support Pages

Plain, accountable, operational.

## Voice Test

Before publishing, ask:

- Would a cautious parent trust this?
- Would an experienced rider respect this?
- Would a buyer understand what to check next?
- Is every claim supported or clearly marked for review?
- Does this sound like an advisor, not a hype store?
