# Target Audience And Personas

## Audience Definition

The brand serves American electric dirt bike buyers who need confidence before purchase.

The audience is not one generic "electric dirt bike shopper." Different buyers have different
fears, use cases, budgets, and proof needs.

## Persona A: The Safety-First Parent

### Profile

- Age: 35-48.
- Buying for a child or early teen.
- Budget: Often $800-$2,500, but willing to pay more for confidence.
- Experience: May not be a dirt bike expert.

### Main Job

Find a bike that fits the child without creating unnecessary safety risk.

### Pain

- Does not know whether age, height, or voltage matters most.
- Worried about speed, brakes, protective gear, and supervision.
- Confused by toy-class bikes versus real youth bikes.

### Fear

Buying a bike that is too fast, too tall, too heavy, or too hard to control.

### Dream

Give the child a fun, confidence-building riding experience without feeling reckless.

### Objections

- Is this safe for my child?
- Is this too powerful?
- What gear is required?
- Can my child actually control it?
- What happens if it breaks?

### Buying Trigger

Birthday, holiday, family land, neighbor/friend influence, youth riding interest, upgrade from a
toy bike.

### Content That Converts

- Parent fit checklist.
- Age versus size explanation.
- Low-voltage guide.
- Safety gear checklist.
- Supervision and riding-area guidance.
- Clear "not ideal for" sections.

## Persona B: The Performance Trail Rider

### Profile

- Age: 22-38.
- Wants trail, practice, private-property riding, or performance.
- Budget: $3,000-$6,500+.
- Experience: May compare Surron, Talaria, Stark, KTM, or gas dirt bikes.

### Main Job

Find a bike that delivers enough performance without sacrificing control, reliability, or support.

### Pain

- Too many claims around speed, range, voltage, and motor power.
- Wants to understand what actually changes ride feel.
- Unsure whether 60V, 72V, wheel size, chassis, and battery claims are comparable.

### Fear

Buying an underpowered bike, a poorly supported bike, or a bike that looks strong on specs but
feels wrong on real terrain.

### Dream

Own an electric dirt bike that feels capable, modern, quiet, and satisfying to ride.

### Objections

- Is this fast enough?
- Is the range real?
- How does it compare to Surron or Talaria?
- Can I get parts?
- Is the frame, suspension, and braking system good enough?

### Buying Trigger

Upgrade from mountain bike, pit bike, mini bike, gas dirt bike, or entry e-moto; seeing social
videos; wanting quiet trail riding.

### Content That Converts

- 60V versus 72V guide.
- Range method explainer.
- Motor and controller guide.
- Brakes/suspension checklist.
- Competitor comparison pages.
- Product fit matrix.

## Persona C: The Practical Landowner

### Profile

- Age: 35-65.
- Ranch, farm, acreage, rural property, hunting land, or utility use.
- Budget: $2,500-$5,500.
- Wants reliability, range, quiet operation, parts, and easy ownership.

### Main Job

Find a quiet, practical off-road electric vehicle for property use.

### Pain

- Not sure whether an electric dirt bike, ATV, UTV, or e-bike fits the job.
- Needs enough range and durability without overbuying.
- Cares about repair and parts more than hype.

### Fear

Buying something fun but impractical, fragile, unsupported, or hard to service.

### Dream

Move around property quietly and efficiently without gas, noise, or complicated maintenance.

### Objections

- Is this durable?
- Can it carry what I need?
- Can I repair it?
- How far will it go in real use?
- What happens after the warranty?

### Buying Trigger

Property work, hunting season, replacing a gas vehicle, wanting quiet mobility.

### Content That Converts

- Terrain-use guide.
- Ownership-cost guide.
- Maintenance checklist.
- Battery care guide.
- Parts and repair pages.
- Support workflow.

## Persona D: The Weekend Explorer

### Profile

- Age: 25-50.
- Recreational buyer.
- Budget: $2,000-$5,000.
- Wants fun, portability, reasonable performance, and lower maintenance.

### Main Job

Find a fun electric dirt bike that fits weekend riding without creating legal or ownership
surprises.

### Pain

- Unsure where riding is allowed.
- Unsure how to transport, charge, store, and maintain the bike.
- Wants fun but does not want to become a mechanic.

### Fear

Buying a bike that is not usable where they live, not practical to transport, or not supported.

### Dream

Have a simple, exciting weekend riding experience with fewer ownership headaches.

### Objections

- Where can I ride it?
- Can I register it?
- How do I transport it?
- How often does it need maintenance?
- Is charging simple?

### Buying Trigger

Weekend hobby, outdoor lifestyle, family recreation, local trails, social videos.

### Content That Converts

- Street legal guide.
- Registration guide.
- Electric versus gas guide.
- Charging guide.
- Transport and storage guidance.
- Beginner buying checklist.

## Persona E: The Dealer Or Wholesale Buyer

### Profile

- Business buyer, local dealer, distributor, rental operator, or service operator.
- Needs margin, reliability, product data, parts, warranty process, and repeat supply.

### Main Job

Evaluate whether the brand is credible enough to sell or support.

### Pain

- Needs clear specs, documentation, MOQ, wholesale process, warranty flow, and parts availability.

### Fear

Taking on a product line that creates support problems.

### Dream

Find a product line with demand, margin, clear support, and dependable supply.

### Objections

- What is the margin?
- Are parts available?
- Who handles warranty?
- What product documents exist?
- Is there protected territory?

### Content That Converts

- Dealer inquiry path.
- Product data sheets.
- Parts and warranty process.
- Support SLAs.
- Real documentation.

## Persona Priority

Launch priority:

1. Safety-first parent.
2. Performance trail rider.
3. Weekend explorer.
4. Practical landowner.
5. Dealer or wholesale buyer.

This priority can change after traffic and conversion data.
