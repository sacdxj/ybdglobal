# Brand Archetype

## Primary Archetype

```text
The Guide / Navigator
```

The brand does not play the hero. The buyer is the hero.

The brand's role is to help the buyer move through uncertainty and choose with confidence.

## Why Guide / Navigator

The electric dirt bike category is confusing:

- Many buyers do not know where to start.
- Parents worry about safety.
- Riders compare voltage and speed without enough context.
- Street-legal claims are complicated.
- Product support, parts, and warranty are unclear.
- Competitor comparisons can be noisy and biased.

The brand wins by guiding buyers through these decisions.

## Archetype Statement

```text
We guide riders through the buying decision so they can choose the right bike with confidence.
```

## Archetype Behavior

The Guide:

- Starts with questions.
- Clarifies the terrain.
- Names the risks.
- Shows the path.
- Explains tradeoffs.
- Refuses unsafe shortcuts.
- Helps the buyer own the decision.

The Navigator:

- Turns scattered information into a route.
- Helps buyers know what to check first, second, and third.
- Creates tools, maps, matrices, and decision flows.

## Secondary Archetypes

### Sage

Used for technical clarity, buying education, range, battery, legal, and comparison pages.

Risk:

If overused, the brand becomes academic and less commercial.

### Caregiver

Used for kids/youth safety, support, warranty, repair, and post-purchase guidance.

Risk:

If overused, the brand becomes too cautious and loses outdoor energy.

### Explorer

Used for terrain, trail, farm, weekend riding, and outdoor lifestyle.

Risk:

If overused, the brand becomes lifestyle-first and may drift away from decision value.

## Rejected Primary Archetypes

### Hero

Rejected because the brand should not frame itself as the champion. The buyer should feel
empowered.

### Outlaw

Rejected because street-legality, youth safety, and battery safety require responsibility.

### Magician

Rejected because the category needs clarity, not mystery.

### Creator

Rejected as primary because the project is not mainly about invention or design culture.

## Brand-As-Guide Voice

Good:

```text
Start with the rider. Then verify terrain, power, range, support, and legal needs.
```

Weak:

```text
This is the ultimate machine for every rider.
```

## Archetype In Website Experience

### Homepage

Should feel like a trailhead:

- Choose your rider.
- Choose your use case.
- Compare your options.
- Verify the risks.

### Buying Guides

Should feel like maps:

- Here are the decision paths.
- Here is what each path means.
- Here is where to go next.

### Product Pages

Should feel like decision checkpoints:

- This fits.
- This does not fit.
- This is proven.
- This needs confirmation.

### Comparison Pages

Should feel like fair route comparisons:

- Path A works for this rider.
- Path B works for that rider.
- This claim needs proof.

## Archetype In Visual Identity

Visual cues should suggest:

- Direction.
- Terrain.
- Clarity.
- Confidence.
- Route finding.
- Readiness.

Avoid:

- Aggressive racing cues.
- Reckless speed cues.
- Toy-like youth imagery.
- Fake extreme adventure.

## Archetype Test

Before publishing, ask:

- Does this guide the buyer or just sell at them?
- Does this make the buyer feel more capable?
- Does this show the next step clearly?
- Does this avoid pretending the brand is the hero?
- Does this help the buyer navigate risk?
